# Business Cycle Analysis

The deliverable identifies the sub-sector's current cycle phase and explains how it changes the subject's demand, margins, investment needs, and position against named alternatives. Different parts of the cycle can move at different speeds.

## Inputs

- The subject: an aVenture URL or a name. The URL wins when both appear.
- Sub-sector (optional): the product, buyer, and geography to assess.
- Context (optional): the reader's decision, comparison period, or horizon.

Apply the designated answer and the context through every step; the defaults below apply only where they are absent. Facts the user supplies are claims to verify.

## 1. Pin the Subject

1. Use available aVenture reads first: the subject's record holds its description, funding rounds, news, and research. With the aVenture MCP server or CLI, an aventure.vc URL resolves in one read (`getEntityLookup`); the aventure skill names the other reads. Make these reads before any web search. Existing research is a hypothesis to verify and date, not the answer. Without an aVenture tool, follow aventure skill §1 step 2. When no matched record results, establish identity from the subject's own site and filings, say so in the report, and never invent record contents.
2. Name the exact entity and its current products, ownership, and operating geography. A parent's capacity or financing is available to the subject only when evidence establishes access to it.
3. When two records fit the name, stop and report both candidates with the fact that separates them.

## 2. Define the Sub-sector

State the boundary in one sentence: the product, the buyer, the job purchased, and the geography. Name the alternatives serving those buyers. When the subject spans economically material sub-sectors with different cycles, assess each separately or limit every conclusion to the one assessed.

Choose comparable dated periods that reveal a turn rather than a single observation. Distinguish seasonality, a temporary shock, and structural adoption from a recurring business cycle. A broad GDP or technology-sector trend is context, not proof of this sub-sector's phase.

## 3. Establish Each Phase

Collect the evidence before naming a phase. For each dimension, state the current condition, its direction, the observation period, the mechanism, and a phase judgment or unknown. Do not force all dimensions into one label.

| Dimension | Evidence to collect |
|---|---|
| Demand | Orders, bookings, shipments, customer budgets, usage, replacement demand, cancellations, and inventories; separate price, volume, and mix where possible |
| Capacity | Utilization, lead times, backlogs, available supply, announced versus operating additions, closures, and delivery constraints |
| Pricing | Realized prices, discounts, contract renewals, input costs, and margins; distinguish list prices from transaction prices |
| Capital | Sector investment, borrowing conditions, financing availability, capital expenditure, and balance-sheet constraints |
| Regulation | Rules proposed, enacted, effective, or enforced; implementation dates and the demand, capacity, cost, or entry mechanism affected |

Use expansion, peak, contraction, trough, or recovery only when the evidence supports that interpretation; label the phase as inference. Regulation has its own implementation stage, not an economic-cycle label. Identify leading, coincident, and lagging indicators where their relationship is established. A financing boom alone does not prove customer demand or a cycle peak.

## 4. Draw the Company Implication

1. Compare the subject with named alternatives on exposure to the same phase: buyer mix, inventory, utilization, pricing power, input dependence, available capital, and regulatory readiness.
2. Explain how each material phase changes revenue, margin, cash needs, or feasible investment for this subject. Separate a sector movement from a company-specific execution problem.
3. Give conditional scenarios over the reader's horizon, defaulting to the next two to three years. Name the dated signal that would confirm a turn and what would disprove it; do not invent a turning date or probability.

## 5. Write the Report

The report's first line is "As of YYYY-MM-DD." Then:

1. Answer: three to five sentences on the sub-sector's phase, the evidence that matters most, and the subject's relative exposure.
2. Sub-sector boundary and comparison periods.
3. Demand, capacity, pricing, capital, and regulation: a table of conditions, dated indicators, phase judgments, and direction, followed only by necessary explanation.
4. The subject against named alternatives: consequences for revenue, margins, cash, and investment.
5. What would change this view: observable signals for the next phase and conditional company implications.
6. Gaps: missing indicators, uncertain phase judgments, and limits on coverage.
7. Before sending, reread the report and move every sentence or table cell that states a number, date, role, customer, or ownership fact without a link in that same sentence or cell into Gaps.

## Evidence Rules

- A search-results page or a listing page (a topic, tag, stream, or index page) is never a source; open the article and cite it.
- Source ladder: filings, regulators, and official statistics; then company primary sources; then dated reporting from established outlets; then analyst and data-provider estimates, labeled by publisher. Cite the filing or issuer's own disclosure, not a repost.
- Read every cited source and cite it at the claim it directly supports. Syndicated reporting is one source, not corroboration. Label estimates, interested-party claims, and your own inferences, and show calculation inputs.
- Align product, geography, unit, period, and measurement method before comparing indicators. Separate publication dates from observation periods; resolve conflicting sources by scope and date, and say how.
- Keep planned capacity apart from operating capacity and proposed rules apart from effective rules. A management target is not observed demand.
- Distinguish not disclosed, not found in the sources searched, not applicable, and conflicting. Never turn an unknown into zero or absence.
- When the research budget runs out, write from what is established and list the rest under Gaps; never infer a missing figure.

## Writing Rules

- Every sentence or table cell that states a number, date, role, customer, or ownership fact links to the page you read for it. A claim you cannot cite moves to Gaps.
- A numeric threshold or target appears only with a cited benchmark; otherwise name the metric without a number.
- Keep the prose under 2,000 words unless the request asks for more; tables do not count toward it. State each finding once; do not repeat a table in prose.
- Each sentence states a fact, a number, a comparison, or a judgment tied to evidence.
- Never write "well-positioned", "poised for growth", "headwinds and tailwinds", "robust", "game-changer", or a closing summary that repeats the answer; state the measure instead. Use "leverage" only in its financial sense.
- Numbers carry currency, unit, period, and an as-of date.
- State uncertainty once, at the claim it affects, with its cause.
